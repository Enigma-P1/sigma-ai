A one-page brief for the reviewer asked to review this project's
grading rubric. Written to be handed over as-is.

## The project, in three sentences

Sigma AI ("Green Belt in a Box") is a free, open-source desktop suite that
walks an untrained person through a real DMAIC improvement project at
Green Belt quality — deterministic statistics engine underneath, AI advisor
on top. Its shipping gate is a literal test: untrained users run real
projects with it, and their output is graded against a Green Belt rubric.
The suite never claims to certify anyone; it teaches and enforces method.

## Why this role exists

The rubric was authored from the ASQ CSSGB (2022) and IASSC/PeopleCert
Green Belt bodies of knowledge by the same effort that built the suite. A
rubric graded only by its own authors would make the evaluation circular —
so the project requires **an independent certified Belt who wrote none of
the content** to review the rubric before it locks (PLAN §6, §9). Findings
are folded in or answered in writing; the rubric moves from DRAFT to locked
on this reviewer's pass.

## Who qualifies

- Certified Lean Six Sigma Green Belt or Black Belt (ASQ, IASSC, or an
  equivalent program with real project history).
- No involvement in authoring this repository's content.
- Comfortable saying "this criterion is wrong" in writing — the review is
  adversarial by design.

## The ask

**Now (milestone 0):** review `docs/green-belt-rubric.md` — 39 grading
items across Define/Measure/Analyze/Improve/Control/Wrap — for method
fidelity and calibration ("acceptable Green Belt work" for a guided first
project, not Black Belt polish). Section 10 of that file lists eight open
calibration questions awaiting your ruling. Spot-check against
`docs/traceability-matrix.md` (the coverage map) as needed. Rough effort:
2–4 hours, async, written notes in whatever form suits.

**Later (final milestone):** apply the locked rubric to a handful of
completed test projects from untrained users (the "high-schooler test"),
grading each phase. A few hours per project, scheduled well in advance.

## What you get

Named credit in the README as the project's independent reviewer (or
anonymity if preferred), on an Apache-2.0 open-source portfolio project.
Compensation, if any, is agreed directly with the project owner.

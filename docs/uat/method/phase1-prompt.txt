Before you touch the software, set the scene — this is your own situation, not
one handed to you.

1. WHO YOU ARE. Job title, industry, size of the operation, how many people
   report to you, how long you have been doing it. Give yourself a name.

2. THE PROBLEM YOU ACTUALLY HAVE. A specific, real, annoying operational
   problem in your workplace. Not a textbook case study — the kind of thing
   that makes you sigh on a Monday. Include roughly what it costs you, in
   whatever units you would actually think in (hours, money, complaints,
   scrapped units, overtime), and how you currently know it is a problem
   (usually: someone complains, or a number on a report someone sends you).

3. WHAT DATA YOU HAVE, REALISTICALLY. Be honest about how bad it is. Most
   supervisors have a spreadsheet someone maintains badly, or nothing.

4. WHAT YOU ARE HOPING THE SOFTWARE DOES FOR YOU. In your words. What would
   make you keep using it, and what would make you close it and never open it
   again.

5. YOUR PLAN FOR THE NEXT HOUR. A numbered list of what you will actually try
   to do in the app, in order, starting from opening it for the first time.
   Be concrete — "make a new project called X", "type in the thing about Y",
   "find whatever tells me Z". Include what you EXPECT to happen at each step,
   so it can be compared with what actually happens. 10-20 steps.

   Assume nothing about the app's layout. You have not seen it.

Someone will drive the app for you, exactly as you describe, and bring you back
screenshots of what happened. So say precisely what you want tried, including
the specific words and numbers you would type in — invent realistic ones.
